This plot shows the **ratio of the matter power spectrum in a modified gravity (MG) model** to that in the standard ΛCDM model,

$$
\frac{P_{\mathrm{MG}}(k)}{P_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(k)}
$$

as a function of wavenumber $k$ (in log scale) for different values of $f_{R0}$ in $f(R)$ gravity.

### Key points in the interpretation:

1. **x-axis ($k$)**

   * This is the comoving wavenumber, related to spatial scale by $\lambda \sim 1/k$.
   * Small $k$ → large scales (hundreds of Mpc).
   * Large $k$ → small scales (a few Mpc or less).

2. **y-axis**

   * Ratio $P_{\mathrm{MG}}(k) / P_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(k)$.
   * If the ratio is **1**, MG behaves like ΛCDM for that $k$.
   * Values above 1 mean MG predicts *more clustering* than ΛCDM.

3. **Parameter $f_{R0}$**

   * This is the present-day value of the scalar degree of freedom in Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ gravity, controlling deviation from GR.
   * Smaller $f_{R0}$ → weaker deviations (closer to GR/ΛCDM).
   * Larger $f_{R0}$ → stronger deviations.

4. **Trends observed**

   * **At small $k$** (large scales): ratios are close to 1 for all $f_{R0}$, meaning MG effects are negligible on large scales.
   * **At larger $k$** (smaller scales): the ratio increases, meaning MG boosts structure growth on small scales.
   * The enhancement grows with $f_{R0}$. For $f_{R0} = 10^{-4}$, the boost can be \~35% at $k \sim 1\, h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$.
   * There is a mild peak before the ratio slightly declines or flattens at the very high $k$ end—this can be related to screening effects or nonlinear suppression.

5. **Physical reason**

   * In $f(R)$ gravity, the effective gravitational force is stronger in unscreened regions, enhancing matter clustering especially on intermediate/small scales.
   * The chameleon mechanism screens gravity on large scales and high-density environments, explaining why the ratio approaches 1 at low $k$.
